# Universal Problem Intake and Solvability

Jarvis accepts a common problem contract before choosing tools or generating solutions. The
contract requires a problem statement and desired outcome, then records evidence, constraints,
stakeholders, proposed actions, success measures, and timing.

The solvability engine returns one of three initial states:

- `needs_definition`: too many critical unknowns remain.
- `ready_for_analysis`: evidence is sufficient for bounded analysis or sandbox work.
- `restricted`: high-stakes or externally consequential action requires qualified review.

This prevents a fluent answer from being confused with a solved problem. Some problems are
temporarily blocked by missing evidence, authority, resources, or technology. Others contain
conflicting goals or cannot be guaranteed. Jarvis records those limits and searches for the
smallest safe next step.

## Standard routing

- Software, GitHub, Linux, and security: reproduce, diagnose, sandbox, test, propose.
- Business and creative strategy: Problem Council, experiments, measurement, review.
- Medical, legal, financial, security, identity, and physical safety: qualified reviewer
  before consequential action.
- Underdefined requests: clarify evidence, limits, stakeholders, and success criteria.

A universal problem solver is built by improving classification, tool selection, verification,
and escalation coverage—not by pretending uncertainty has disappeared.
