# Video Book: A reviewable assignment, one decision at a time

AI Jarvis and the Empowered Human Workforce — Chapter 6 companion.

Status: original AI-assisted storyboard draft. Direct sources: the accompanying chapter and its narration adaptation. No video book, narration track, animation, captions, or interactive player has been rendered. The scene windows are planning estimates, not measured synchronization.

## Treatment

Use a chapter-based teaching film with eight scenes corresponding to narration sections N1–N8. Show only the information needed for the current decision. The visual sequence should make changes and dependencies understandable without requiring the viewer to read a code listing or know a specific AI platform.

The interaction is a proposed pause-and-practice treatment. A standard video can invite the viewer to pause; a future interactive edition could collect answers after a separate implementation and privacy review. This storyboard does not implement a player or collect learner data.

## Detailed direction for the key scene

VB5 should carry the central teaching burden. Begin with three plain documents named “chapter A,” “narration A,” and “storyboard A.” Draw a labeled connection from the chapter to the narration and from both to the storyboard. Say that the labels represent the exact versions used.

Next, show the corrected chapter B beside chapter A. Keep the old version visible rather than replacing it so quickly that the viewer cannot understand the change. The narration remains labeled A. Add the words “review required: source changed” beside that connection.

Show two separate actions: first “record new source bytes,” then “inspect and adapt the narration.” Explain that changing the recorded hash does not perform the second action. After the narration is revised, show why the storyboard also needs comparison with its narration source.

Finish with a question: “What would you check before calling this version ready for a reader trial?” Hold the diagram for a planned five-second reflection pause. The producer should measure whether the actual sequence gives viewers enough time; the plan's pause is not evidence of a usability test.

## Story and sound

The tone should be patient and concrete. Characters appear through simple task cards or an original illustrated workspace, not through impersonations of real employees. Use silence where it helps a learner think. Music is optional and requires a documented source and permission before use. No third-party soundtrack, actor image, or voice clone is supplied.

Keep a clean spoken track for accessibility review. Read the important meaning of each diagram aloud. A later descriptive-audio adaptation may be appropriate depending on the finished visuals; the decision should follow inspection of the actual media.

## Chapter navigation and companion materials

Proposed navigation labels: Assignment; Evidence; Brief; Roles; Change; Formats; Costs; Your Turn. Create chapter markers only after the edited recording establishes the actual timings. Link the published chapter and worksheet only after their destination and release version are verified.

A learner should be able to follow the lesson through the transcript and static diagram descriptions if a video player is unavailable. The plain-text chapter and narration provide the starting material for that alternate route; final accessibility still needs review of the exported files and player.
