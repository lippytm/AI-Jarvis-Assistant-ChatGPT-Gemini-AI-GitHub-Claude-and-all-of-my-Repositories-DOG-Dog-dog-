# Workforce pilot: production and reader experiment

Status: a proposed experiment with a real local source packet. No participants have been recruited, messages sent, paid offers activated, or learning outcomes measured by this document.

## Decision the experiment should support

Decide whether the source-version lesson is understandable and useful enough to justify editing and producing a small recorded companion. The larger 350+ page book remains the long-form goal. The pilot tests one teaching unit before expanding its production burden.

The proposed first audience is a small studio operator, educator, or team lead who has tried an AI tool but has not built a formal content-production process. Readers should not need a paid model account. The technical exercise requires a suitable Python environment; a paper version can use the fictional version labels and dependency diagram, with that different exercise recorded separately.

## Work in dependency order

| Stage | Concrete work | Evidence and decision |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Source preparation | Build the supplied four-asset packet and verify it. | Matching brief/source/profile/config records and visible remaining review state. |
| 2. Editorial review | Compare the chapter with its stated learner outcome; compare each adaptation with its exact parent. | Findings tied to source hashes; explain corrections and unresolved questions. |
| 3. Small reader trial | After consent and suitable arrangements, invite a small exploratory group to perform the lesson. | Observed completion, explanations, confusion, correction time, and accessibility needs. |
| 4. Revision | Change the lesson in response to specific observations, then recheck affected adaptations. | New packet, change notes, and comparison with prior findings. |
| 5. Recorded companion | Select voice/visual sources and produce the smallest useful reviewed media edition. | Actual recordings, captions/transcript, rights evidence, listening/viewing review. |
| 6. Release decision | Select the actual account, edition, audience, access terms, and publication action. | Exact artifact and destination review; a separately verified release result if executed. |

The sequence is a proposal, not a schedule that is running in the background. A person must assign the editorial and reader-review roles. No names, approvals, or account permissions are fabricated in the supplied brief.

## A small exploratory reader trial

An initial group of five to eight willing readers can reveal usability problems worth investigating. That proposed size is a practical planning choice, not a statistically justified proof of effectiveness. Preserve every attempted result, including incomplete attempts. Do not turn a small favorable response into a claim about all businesses or workers.

Give readers a baseline task with a short source and two adaptations. Ask them which version was reviewed and what would need attention after one source changes. Then offer the lesson and a comparable task using different fictional content. Change task order across participants where feasible and record the order; otherwise familiarity with the first task may affect the second.

Keep assistance visible. A participant who receives a hint has a different observation from one who explains the result independently. Record hints, technical setup problems, breaks, and accessibility adjustments. Compare like with like and describe limitations instead of combining unlike attempts into one success figure.

## Observation record

Use a local record with a participant code chosen for the trial. Avoid collecting personal information that the teaching decision does not need. Decide access, retention, and deletion arrangements before collecting identifiable responses. The current software does not collect reader data or implement a study database.

| Observation | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Task version and order | Makes the comparison understandable and reveals practice effects. |
| Prior familiarity and relevant setup | Explains differences in the starting point without inventing a fixed learner type. |
| Setup, reading, execution, review, and correction minutes | Captures the whole task cost rather than only drafting time. |
| Source version identified correctly | Checks the specific skill the lesson aims to teach. |
| Direct and indirect dependencies explained | Shows whether the reader understands the graph beyond a command's output. |
| Distinction between verified text and released media | Checks a central scope concept. |
| Hints and corrections required | Makes assistance and failure recovery visible. |
| Reader's explanation on a new example | Provides evidence of application beyond repeating the original wording. |
| Confusing sentence or inaccessible step | Gives the editor a concrete revision target. |
| Unfinished attempt and reason | Preserves evidence that a success-only summary would lose. |

Do not populate these fields with invented trial results. The checked-in fictional chapter team is not a participant cohort.

## Proposed revise/continue rules

Before the trial, ask the responsible editor to agree the decision criteria. Candidate rules for discussion are: revise any instruction that causes a participant to mistake a draft for a release; investigate any source-change failure the reader cannot explain after the exercise; simplify repeated points of confusion; and document unresolved accessibility barriers before making the lesson a required route.

Consider recording the companion when the editor can identify a clear teaching contribution from audio/video and the reader observations support that choice. A passing software check alone does not answer that question. If the lesson works well in text, its next improvement may be a clearer exercise rather than more media.

## Cost and demand evidence

The economics file contains four hypothetical unit counts, not sales targets or demand measurements. The assumed $9 price and 5% fee are illustrative. The public fixture is freely visible; a future paid edition would need to describe the additional product, access, support, and terms accurately.

Record actual production time and cash expenses if the project proceeds. Keep those observations separate from the hypothetical input file so that changing an assumption cannot overwrite what happened. Track editing, review, recording, correction, support, and acquisition effort. The v1 calculator evaluates assumptions only and does not provide an actuals ledger or accounting system.

An expression of interest is different from a completed purchase. A purchase is different from a retained sale after refunds. A favorable reader comment is different from demonstrated learning. Decide which evidence the next business decision requires before interpreting any of those observations.
